# The Reshaping of NFL Offense, 2010–2026: What Structural Change Means for Fantasy Football Value

## TL;DR
- The NFL became a spread-passing, 11-personnel league between roughly 2013 and 2018 — but the defining story of 2022–2025 is a **partial reversal**: three-WR sets fell to ~56.4% in 2025 (from a 2023 peak of ~62–63%), 12-personnel hit century-highs (~22%), and the "workhorse" RB returned (six 300-carry backs in 2024 vs. zero in 2023).
- The single most durable structural change for fantasy is the **collapse of the rookie-WR development curve**: since 2020, first-round wideouts (Jefferson, Chase, Nabers, Thomas Jr.) and even Day 3 outliers (Nacua) produce immediately, which — combined with RB injury attrition — pushed WRs up draft boards and birthed the "RB Dead Zone."
- Practical upshot: elite RBs regained genuine first-round value in 2024–2025 (Hero-RB), the tight-end position bifurcated into a tiny elite tier plus rookie exceptions (LaPorta, Bowers, McBride), scheme attachment (Shanahan/McVay, Ben Johnson trees) is a real projection input, and rule/scheduling changes (17 games, dynamic kickoff) inflate counting-stat baselines while scoring itself plateaued.

## Key Findings
1. **Passing/11-personnel rise happened ~2013–2018, then partially reversed 2022–2025.** 11-personnel usage climbed from a base by 2010 (~55% three-WR snaps) to a peak around 62–63% (2023), then fell to ~56.4% in 2025. 12-personnel rose to ~22% (2024–2025), the highest of the 2000s. This is an active, ongoing reversal, not a plateau.
2. **The workhorse RB "died" 2015–2023 and demonstrably returned in 2024.** Six RBs hit 300+ carries in 2024 (first time since 2010) after zero in 2023; 250+-carry backs jumped to 12. Most analysts now agree the "workhorse is dead" narrative was overstated/reversed, though they disagree on whether to pay up.
3. **TE bifurcated:** a tiny elite tier (Kelce, then McBride/Bowers/Kittle) plus a new phenomenon of instantly productive rookie TEs (LaPorta 2023, Bowers 2024) that breaks the old "Year 3 breakout" heuristic.
4. **Rookie WR curve is the clearest structural break:** immediate production since ~2020 is now the norm, driving WRs up draft boards; analysts dispute whether it's durable structure or draft-class/QB noise.
5. **Coaching trees have measurable fantasy fingerprints:** the Shanahan/McVay tree (motion, play-action, YAC, condensed splits), the Reid West Coast tree (spread targets, low concentration), and the ascendant Ben Johnson tree.
6. **Rule/schedule changes inflated baselines** (17th game since 2021, dynamic kickoff 2024–25) while league scoring peaked around 24.8 PPG (2020/2021) and settled to ~22.9–23.0 in 2024–2025.

## Details

### 1. The Rise of the Passing Game and 11 Personnel — and its 2022–2025 reversal

**WHEN.** The spread revolution reached the NFL around 2010; by 2010, 11-personnel (1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WR) was already the base grouping, and from 2014–2017 every NFL team took the majority of its snaps from 11 personnel (Pro Football Insiders). Three-or-more-WR formations rose from ~55% of snaps in 2010 to ~65% at peak (NFL Football Operations). The inflection toward reversal began around 2022, coinciding with the spread of two-high safety shells (popularized by Vic Fangio and his disciples), and accelerated through 2025.

**MAGNITUDE (year-by-year).**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | ~"norm by 2010" (~55% 3WR) | — | 22.0 | 236.2 |
| 2013 | — | — | 23.4 | 252.3 |
| 2015 | ~64–65% (peak era) | — | — | 259.2 (modern peak) |
| 2020 | ~60% | — | 24.8 (record) | 254.9 |
| 2022 | 60.5% | 17.7% | 23.0 → 21.9 (see note) | 234.4 |

(Sources: SumerSports via Corbin Young; Sharp Football Analysis; StatMuse league tables mirroring Pro Football Reference; NFL Football Operations. Note: "11 personnel %" and "3+ WR rate" are related but differ by charting provider — Sharp's 3-WR rate was 62.6% in 2024, "lowest since 2019"; PFF reported neutral pass rates hit a multi-year low of 57.44% over 2024–2025.)

**Yards per attempt / completion %.** Completion percentage rose across the era, but efficiency dipped in the two-high era: league passer rating fell from 93.6 in 2021 to 90.8 in 2022, and yards per play of 5.43 in 2022 was the 2nd-lowest in eight seasons (Fox/AP). Passing peaked in 2015 (259.2 ypg per team), NOT 2013 as often assumed — the 2010s were the passing-peak decade (249.2 ypg average) and the 2020s have pulled back to ~237.9.

**STILL CHANGING?** Actively reversing. Warren Sharp documented the "run game renaissance": in 2024 the dropback rate was 59.5% (only the 2nd time in a decade below 60%) and 35.5% of yardage came via the run (2nd-highest since 2008). 12-personnel usage set 2000s highs in 2024 and 2025; 13-personnel rose to 5.6% in 2025 (Rams led at 30.5%). PFF projects pass rates "could continue to decline… though the recent trend may also begin to level off." The most extreme individual data points: Atlanta under Arthur Smith ran 11 personnel just 16% in 2023; the Rams played 100% 11-personnel in their Super Bowl LVI win (2021) but fell to 16th in 11-personnel usage by 2025 while jumping to 1st in 13-personnel.

### 2. RB Committee Usage and the Collapse — and Return — of the Workhorse

**WHEN.** The workhorse decline is a long arc: 13 RBs had 300+ carries in 2003; the number fell through the 2010s and bottomed at ZERO in 2023. The committee era peaked ~2015–2023. The reversal inflection is 2024.

**MAGNITUDE — RBs with 300+ carries:**

| Season | 300+ carries | Names |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 2 | Henry, Elliott |
| 2020 | 2 | Henry (378), Cook (312) |
| 2021 | 2 | Taylor (332), Harris (307) |
| 2022 | 3 | Henry, Chubb, Jacobs |
| 2023 | 0 | — (bottom) |
| 2024 | 6 | Barkley (345), Henry (325), Kyren Williams (316), Bijan Robinson (304), Jonathan Taylor (303), Josh Jacobs (301) |
| 2025 | 4 | Taylor (323) led; three others |

2024 was the first time SIX backs hit 300+ carries since 2010 (DraftSharks). The 250+-carry count jumped to 12 in 2024 — Colts.com, citing Sharp data, notes "there were 11 [with 250+] in 2016, and then no more than eight in a season until 2024 (12)." The league finished 2024 with 16 1,000-yard rushers (Fox Sports).

**RB draft-capital collapse & 2023 free agency.** The market bottomed in 2023: Saquon Barkley, Josh Jacobs and Tony Pollard were all franchise-tagged (~$10.1M) rather than signed long-term; Ezekiel Elliott and Dalvin Cook became cap casualties; Derrick Henry tweeted "just take the RB position out the game then" (The Ringer). The partial recovery came fast: Barkley — who played 2023 on a $10.1M adjusted franchise tag — signed a 3-year/$37.75M Eagles deal in 2024 ($26M fully guaranteed at signing, up to $46.75M; per ESPN's Adam Schefter) and delivered a 2,005-yard rushing season on 345 carries, becoming the 9th player to reach 2,000. Henry got a 2-year/$30M extension in 2025 (largest ever for a RB over 30). Rookie RB draft capital also recovered — Bijan Robinson (8th, 2023), Jahmyr Gibbs (12th, 2023), Ashton Jeanty (2025, top-6 pick, "the chosen one").

**Receiving usage / scoring-format sensitivity.** DraftSharks: over five years one rush = 0.65 PPR points, while a target is worth far more, so pass-catching backs (McCaffrey, Gibbs) hold PPR value even in committees. In 2024 the six 300-carry backs finished RB1, RB3, RB4, RB7 and RB17 — carries drove them, but receiving separates PPR value.

**DISAGREEMENT (flag).** Analysts split sharply:
- *Workhorse is back / pay up (Hero-RB):* Fox Sports ("demise greatly exaggerated"), Stadium Rant ("RBBC is dead").
- *Workhorse is back but ignore him in PPR:* DraftSharks ("The Workhorse Runner Is Back. Here's Why You Should Ignore Him") argues catches still win full-PPR.
- *Zero-RB is dead / has underperformed:* FTN's "The Death of Zero RB" (2025) shows the strategy hasn't paid off in 2024–2025.
- *Zero-RB lives:* NBC's Shawn Siegele and Fantasy Points argued 2025 was a prime Zero-RB setup ("year of the WR").

### 3. The Changing TE Role

**WHEN/MAGNITUDE.** TE target share concentrated at the top. Trey McBride's 27.1% on-field target share in 2024 was the highest by a qualified TE since Mark Andrews; McBride commanded 31.7% of Arizona targets when on the field. In 2025 he broke Zach Ertz's single-season TE receptions record with 126 catches for 1,239 yards and 11 TDs (surpassing Ertz's 116 from 2018), and became the first TE ever with 100+ catches in back-to-back seasons (111 in 2024). The move/receiving TE (Kelce, McBride, Bowers, Pitts) now dominates fantasy value over the in-line blocker.

**Rookie TE production — the broken heuristic.** Historically rookie TEs were "persona non grata" (Yahoo). That broke: Sam LaPorta (2023, 34th pick) set the rookie-TE fantasy-scoring record at 14.1 PPG; Brock Bowers (2024, 13th pick) caught 112 passes for 1,194 yards and 5 TDs — breaking Mike Ditka's 1961 rookie-TE yardage record and becoming the first rookie TE named First-Team All-Pro since Jeremy Shockey in 2002 — and finished a top-3 fantasy TE. In 2025 two more first-round rookie TEs arrived (Colston Loveland 10th, Tyler Warren 14th — Warren finished rookie TE1 with 76/817).

**"Year 3 breakout" heuristic — does data support it?** McBride fits it (2022 rookie → 2024 breakout), but LaPorta and Bowers destroyed it by breaking out as rookies. The heuristic is weakening; the market now drafts rookie TEs with real capital. CBS notes scheme matters more than service time: Drew Petzing's Arizona offenses threw 27.5% of passes to TEs; Kevin Stefanski's Browns 27.2%.

**12-personnel effect.** Rising 12-personnel (~22%) is a double-edged sword: it raises TE snaps/targets in aggregate (10,222 two-TE plays in 2024, the first time above 10,000 since 2015) but can split target share across two TEs, muddying individual value.

### 4. Rookie WR Usage Curves, Pre- vs Post-2020 — the clearest structural break

**WHEN.** Two inflection points: the 2014 class (Beckham, Evans, Benjamin) first cracked the old "wait 2–3 years" rule; the 2020+ classes made immediate stardom the norm.

**MAGNITUDE.** Seven WRs cleared 1,000 rookie yards over five years (Nacua, A.J. Brown, Chase, Jefferson, Olave, Waddle, Wilson), with near-misses (Higgins, Lamb, DeVonta Smith, St. Brown). First-round WRs finishing WR12-or-better as rookies: OBJ (2014), Jefferson (2020, WR7), Chase (2021, WR5), Waddle (2021, WR12), Nabers (2024, WR6), Brian Thomas Jr. (2024, WR5). Puka Nacua (5th round, 2023) finished WR5 as a rookie — the lone 5th-rounder on that 20-year list. Jefferson set the rookie receiving-yards record; Malik Nabers set the record for receptions in a player's first 10 games (75, breaking OBJ's 71).

**Proposed explanations (per theScore, ETR, Yahoo):** college spread/pro-style concepts; 11-personnel meaning more 3-WR snaps available; simplified route trees; earlier specialization; more early draft capital on WRs; better development pipelines.

**DISAGREEMENT (flag).** This is a live structural-vs-noise debate:
- ETR's Jack Miller ("Are Rookie Wide Receivers Becoming Too Expensive?") warns the Day-3 hit rate remains poor: of 16 regularly-drafted Day 3 WRs (2021–25), only 18.8% outperformed veterans — Nacua may be "a once-in-a-generation outlier."
- The Fantasy Footballers note the rookie-WR ADP-vs-finish trendline "tells a softer story… a slight signal, but also a ton of noise."
- Consensus that survives: **first-round draft capital is genuinely predictive of immediate rookie WR production; late-round hits remain lottery tickets.**

### 5. Offensive Coordinator Trees and Statistical Fingerprints

**Shanahan/McVay (wide zone) tree** — the dominant tree. Members entering 2026 (Fantasy Points): 49ers, Rams, Packers (M. LaFleur), Vikings (O'Connell), Dolphins (McDaniel), Texans, Jaguars (Coen), Bengals (Zac Taylor), Cardinals, Chargers, Raiders, Eagles, Seahawks, Buccaneers. Fingerprints: league-leading pre-snap motion (Shanahan's 49ers led the NFL in motion every year since 2017, peaking as high as ~69–87% in individual games; McVay top-8) and play-action (year-to-year correlation 0.62 for play-action, 0.75 for motion — highly sticky and coach-controlled), condensed splits, YAC-heavy passing, and flexible personnel (Rams shifted from 11-heavy to league-leading 13-personnel in 2025). Fantasy implication: efficient per-play production, but the scheme can suppress a single alpha unless personnel forces concentration (e.g., Nacua post-Kupp).

**Reid West Coast tree** (Pederson, Nagy, Bieniemy; Reid himself out of the Holmgren/Walsh line). Fingerprints: historically high neutral pass rate, spread targets (receiving "shared between multiple players rather than relying on a single dominant receiver"), heavy RB passing-game usage. Fantasy caution: target democratization suppresses weekly ceilings unless the offense is forced to concentrate (Mahomes→Kelce in shootouts).

**Sean Payton tree:** historically high RB target share (27.4% of Saints targets to RBs in 2019, 4th-highest in the league).

**Ben Johnson (Detroit) tree** — ascendant. Johnson's 2022–2024 Lions offenses finished top-5 in total offense each year (No. 2 in 2024), led the NFL with 30.1 PPG and 402.2 net yds/g over 2023–24, and produced 70 offensive TDs in 2024 with five 1,000-all-purpose-yard players (Goff, Gibbs, Montgomery, St. Brown, Jameson Williams). Johnson became Bears HC (Jan 2025), going 11-6 and winning the NFC North in year one. The Ringer frames his scheme as a novel blend of Reid's spread West Coast passing and Payton's run game. Diaspora: Liam Coen (McVay tree) to Jaguars HC; the 2025–26 hiring cycle drew heavily from these offensive trees.

**Scheme-change predictive value.** Establish The Run and DraftSharks publish annual "coaching change impact" pieces because a play-caller swap materially moves projections. Example: Atlanta hired the Rams' staff (Zac Robinson OC) in 2024, and Sharp predicted "the largest year-over-year shift in offensive personnel deployment we've seen in recent history" — the Falcons went from under 15% to 94% early-down first-half 11-personnel. PFF: offenses that lean on play-action and pre-snap motion "create more efficient opportunities… boosting per-play fantasy production," so fantasy managers should prioritize players attached to such playcallers.

### 6. Rule Changes Affecting Scoring

**Arc of major changes:**
- **2011:** kickoff moved to the 35 → more touchbacks.
- **2010s:** defenseless-receiver and illegal-contact emphasis aided passing.
- **2018:** helmet rule, roughing-the-passer emphasis, and new kickoff formation rules.
- **2020:** playoff field expanded to 14 teams; scoring hit a record 24.8 PPG per team (12,692 total points and 1,473 TDs, both NFL records; NFL.com/ProFootballTalk).
- **2021:** 17th regular-season game added (biggest scheduling change since 1978's move to 16 games).
- **2024:** "dynamic kickoff" (XFL-style) + hip-drop tackle ban. Kickoff returns rose from a record-low 21.8% (2023) to 32.8% (2024); 332 more returns; average post-kickoff start moved to the 29.7-yard line (best of the millennium). All hip-drop tackles now draw a 15-yard penalty and automatic first down.
- **2025:** dynamic kickoff made permanent; touchback moved from the 30 to the 35 (owners cited a 43% reduction in kickoff concussions); trailing teams can declare an onside kick anytime; regular-season OT aligned with postseason; the **tush-push ban failed by two votes** (the play stays legal — the Eagles ran it 39 times in 2024, converting first downs on 33).
- **2024 scoring scare → rebound:** scoring fell to 21.8 PPG in 2023 (lowest of the span) and looked worse early in 2024 (21.4 PPG through Week 2, the lowest since 2006), but rebounded to a 22.9 full-season figure; 2025 was ~23.0. Passing per team was 233.5 ypg in 2024, down ~20 yards from the 2020 peak of 254.9.

**17-game effect on fantasy/records.** The extra game inflates counting-stat baselines: 2021 projections had 27 QBs over 4,000 yards and marks like 1,000 rushing/receiving yards losing meaning (≈59 ypg suffices). Yet the marquee 16-game records largely survived — Peyton Manning's 5,477 pass yards (2013), Eric Dickerson's 2,105 rush yards (1984), and Calvin Johnson's 1,964 receiving yards (2012) all still stood as of 2024. Tom Brady (5,316, 2021) and Mahomes (5,250, 2022) set personal highs but were less efficient. For season-long fantasy, the 17th game raises total-points thresholds ~6% and adds a full extra week.

**Fantasy baseline implications.** Rising 12/13-personnel and the run renaissance compress the flex-worthy WR pool (PFF: neutral pass rates at a multi-year low shift baseline touches to RBs), while the dynamic kickoff modestly improves field position and thus scoring opportunity. Net: positional-value math has shifted back slightly toward RBs since 2023 even as the long-run passing tilt remains intact.

## Recommendations

**Draft strategy (staged):**
1. **Treat elite RBs as legitimate first-round assets again (2024–2026).** The workhorse return is real (6 300-carry backs in 2024, 12 with 250+). Hero-RB — one bell-cow early, then hammer WR — is the highest-floor build in the current environment. *Benchmark that would flip this:* if 300-carry backs fall back to ≤2 for two straight seasons, revert to WR-heavy/Zero-RB.
2. **Prioritize WRs in Rounds 2–4 and avoid the RB Dead Zone (Rounds 4–7)** unless the back has a defined receiving role. In PPR/half-PPR, pass-catching backs and WR volume are the safest points.
3. **At TE, either pay for the tiny elite tier (McBride/Bowers/Kittle) or punt** — the middle is fungible. Do NOT reflexively fade rookie TEs anymore if they have first-round draft capital and a target-friendly scheme (Petzing/Stefanski/Johnson trees throw 27%+ to TEs).
4. **Draft rookie WRs by draft capital.** First-round rookie WRs are now strong immediate bets; Day 3 rookie WRs remain lottery tickets (~19% hit rate) — chase them only in best-ball/deep formats.
5. **Use scheme as a tiebreaker.** Upgrade players entering Shanahan/McVay or Ben Johnson offenses (motion + play-action = efficiency); fade target-democratizing Reid-tree WR2/WR3s for weekly ceilings.

**Format sensitivity:** In full PPR, weight receiving backs and WR volume heavily; in standard scoring, elite rushing/TD workhorses (Henry-type) regain relative value; Superflex/2-QB inflates QB and elite-TE premiums.

**Thresholds to watch (2026 season):** the 11-personnel rate (if it keeps falling below ~55%, further devalue WR3s and boost TEs/RBs); the number of 300-carry backs; and whether a Day-3 rookie WR again finishes top-12 (which would strengthen the "structural" case for the rookie-WR shift).

## Caveats
- **Personnel percentages vary by charting provider.** SumerSports, Sharp, PFF and Next Gen Stats each produce slightly different 11/12-personnel numbers ("11 personnel %" vs "3+ WR rate" are different cuts). I used the SumerSports series for year-over-year consistency and flagged Sharp/PFF variants inline. Treat exact decimals as provider-specific.
- **2020/2021 scoring-record conflict:** the subagent's StatMuse table shows 2020 = 24.8 PPG (record) and 2021 = 23.0, but Fox/AP reporting described 2021 as the "record 24.8" that "dropped to 23 in 2022." Sources disagree on which season owns the 24.8 record; all agree the peak was ~24.8 and scoring settled to ~22.9–23.0 by 2024–2025.
- **2025 passing yards/game per team could not be confirmed** to a single authoritative figure; 2025 PPG (~23.0) comes from a StatMuse league table mirroring Pro Football Reference and should be re-confirmed at PFR.
- **The 2020 passing figure discrepancy:** league per-team passing average was 254.9 ypg in 2020; a "240.2" figure cited by NBC is a different cut (likely a net or subset measure).
- **Rookie-WR aggregate PPG pre-2020 vs post-2020** could not be verified to a single clean league-average stat; the case rests on finish-based evidence (1,000-yard rookies, WR12+ rookie finishes) rather than a PPG series.
- **Several fantasy-analytics claims rely on single sources** (e.g., DraftSharks' 0.65 PPR points/rush; the SumerSports personnel series relayed via a Substack aggregation). Where possible I corroborated with a second source.
- Pro Football Reference and TeamRankings blocked automated fetching during research; raw historical numbers were sourced via StatMuse (which mirrors PFR) and reputable secondary reporting.
- The "run game renaissance" and "workhorse return" are recent (2024–2025) and could regress; PFF itself hedges that the pass-rate decline "may begin to level off," so the reversal's durability is not yet established.
